# Motion rules the templates follow

The templates already apply these; use them to explain choices or when the user asks why.

1. **Vector law at every cut.** The outgoing scene leaves on the same axis and direction the
   next one arrives (default: to the left), with mirrored power4 eases so speed matches at the
   cut. The cut lands mid-motion. No plain crossfades.
2. **Reserved directions.** Left is "next beat". Up (`push-up`) means a conclusion or reveal.
   `zoom` pushes deeper into the same idea. Do not alternate directions without a reason.
3. **Sustained motion.** Every scene keeps a slow push, so no frame is dead.
4. **Entrances ease out, exits ease in.** Stagger stays under 0.5 s in total. Text arrives word
   by word with a short blur.
5. **Springs with a small overshoot (about 6%)** for pops: logo, buttons, callouts, icons.
6. **The cursor is a protagonist.** It is oversized, enters from off-screen (never fades in),
   glides to the target, clicks (squash and ripple), the camera zooms to the click, then it
   leaves and carries the eye into the next scene.
7. **Music first.** Cuts snap to the beat of the track; a whoosh plays on each moving cut and
   a click sound on each click.
8. **Opaque stage.** Transitions never show a white flash.
9. **Personality sets the tempo.** Premium is 15% slower, energetic 20% faster.

Sources (rules adapted, not copied): LottieFiles motion-design-skill (MIT): timing and easing
tables, choreography. HeyGen HyperFrames skills (Apache-2.0): motion-doctrine vector law,
seam-craft and oversized-cursor. AbubakrChan/product-launch-motion (MIT): music-first timing
and synthesized UI sound.
